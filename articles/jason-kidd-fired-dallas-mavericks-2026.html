Jason Kidd Fired by the Dallas Mavericks: Why It Happened, the Luka Trade Shadow, and Who Coaches Cooper Flagg Next

The Dallas Mavericks parted ways with head coach Jason Kidd on May 19, 2026. New president Masai Ujiri made the call with four years and over $40 million remaining on Kidd's contract. Here is the complete story of why it happened and what the franchise does next.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 20, 2026·Updated 3h ago·6 min read·👁 32,819 views
Share:

Jason Kidd is no longer the head coach of the Dallas Mavericks. The team announced Tuesday evening, May 19, 2026, that it was parting ways with Kidd in what it officially described as a "mutual decision" — just two weeks after new team president Masai Ujiri arrived in Dallas with a mandate to evaluate every aspect of the organization. Kidd had four years and more than $40 million remaining on his contract. Dallas is prepared to eat that cost entirely.

Jason Kidd at Dallas Mavericks — By the Numbers
  • Tenure: June 2021 – May 19, 2026 (5 seasons)
  • Overall record: 205–205
  • Playoff record: 22–18
  • Best finish: 2024 NBA Finals (lost to Boston Celtics)
  • 2025–26 record: 26–56 (without Luka Dončić)
  • Contract remaining: 4 years, $40M+
  • Record with Dončić: 136–87
  • Record without Dončić: 69–118

Why was Jason Kidd fired? The real reasons behind the split

The official line — "mutual decision," "right moment for a new direction" — tells you almost nothing. The actual reasons are layered and go back well before Ujiri's arrival.

The central issue is the Luka Dončić trade. In February 2025, Dallas dealt the face of the franchise — one of the five best players in basketball — to the Los Angeles Lakers in a package centered on Anthony Davis. It was one of the most unpopular transactions in modern NBA history. Davis barely played in Dallas and was subsequently traded to the Washington Wizards. The architect of the deal, general manager Nico Harrison, was fired in November 2025 as fan fury reached a breaking point.

Kidd's role in the Dončić trade has never been fully resolved. He maintained consistently that he was "kept out of the loop until the 11th hour." In early April 2026, minority owner Mark Cuban appeared on a podcast and said directly that Kidd was involved in the decision. The truth likely lies somewhere between the two accounts — but the ambiguity was enough to erode trust throughout the organization.

"The bottom line is that this is Masai Ujiri's franchise." — Dallas reporter Tim Cato, the day of the firing

The front office power struggle that sealed Kidd's fate

After Harrison's firing, Kidd reportedly lobbied to be promoted to president of basketball operations — a Brad Stevens-style transition from the bench to the front office. Owner Patrick Dumont informed Kidd directly that he would not be considered for the role. Then Ujiri was hired on May 5, 2026, in the biggest outside hire in Dallas basketball operations history.

Ujiri was noncommittal about Kidd's future at his introductory press conference but signaled the writing on the wall with a careful choice of words: he noted that the coach in place had typically survived his arrival in Toronto and Denver. This time, he said, he was "starting fresh." Every reporter in the room understood what that meant. Kidd was kept out of the loop during the Ujiri hiring process entirely.

According to ESPN's Tim MacMahon, Kidd and Ujiri simply did not see eye to eye. The new president wanted complete authority — as he'd had in Toronto when he built a championship team around his own vision. Kidd, who had accumulated significant influence within the organization over five seasons, represented the previous regime rather than the new one.

What Jason Kidd accomplished in Dallas

The fairest assessment of Kidd's tenure requires separating what he could control from what he could not. When he arrived in June 2021, he inherited a roster built around Dončić — already a two-time first-team All-NBA selection at 22 years old. In his first season, Kidd took Dallas further than nearly anyone expected: a stunning Game 7 road win over the Phoenix Suns in the second round, then a five-game Western Conference Finals loss to Golden State.

The 2024 NBA Finals run — against the Boston Celtics — was the highlight of his coaching tenure and one of the more unexpected deep playoff runs of the decade. Dončić was at his absolute peak, and Kidd had constructed a defense that gave Boston genuine problems. Dallas lost the series 4–1, but the journey there was real.

The final chapter was more complicated. After the Dončić trade left Dallas at 26–56 in 2025–26, Kidd's most genuinely impressive work was arguably his development of rookie Cooper Flagg — the No. 1 overall pick who won the 2026 Kia Rookie of the Year Award with 21.0 points, 6.7 rebounds, and 4.5 assists per game. Kidd coached him "like a superstar" according to multiple reports, building the entire offense around Flagg's growth. The relationship between coach and young star was strong enough that even some of Kidd's critics softened toward the end.

Who is the next Mavericks coach? The candidates

Dallas has immediately opened a coaching search under Ujiri's direction. The Mavericks described it as "thorough and disciplined." The most prominent names currently connected to the opening:

Billy Donovan is considered one of the top available candidates. The former Chicago Bulls coach has Finals experience, championship pedigree from his time at Oklahoma City, and a reputation for player development. Tom Thibodeau, fresh off the Knicks' current Eastern Conference Finals run, is another experienced option — though his demanding style may or may not align with Ujiri's vision for a young team.

Dallas could also pursue a younger, first-time head coach who can grow with Flagg over a long arc — the kind of hire Ujiri made in Toronto when he bet on a relatively unknown Dwane Casey and eventually built toward a championship. The franchise's next direction, whoever that coach is, will be defined almost entirely by what Flagg becomes. Kyrie Irving is also expected to return healthy next season, which adds immediate competitive pressure to get the hire right.

What this means for Cooper Flagg

For Flagg, the coaching change arrives at a delicate moment. His first NBA season showed a player already operating at a high level — poised, skilled, and clearly built for the long run. The relationship he developed with Kidd was valuable, and rebuilding that trust with a new coach takes time. The good news for Flagg and for Dallas fans: his ceiling is exceptional, and the talent is there regardless of who draws up the plays.

Ujiri has built championship programs before. The Raptors were a mid-level team when he arrived and won a title in his eighth year. Dallas is betting he can do something similar around Flagg — and Tuesday's firing of Kidd, expensive and disruptive as it is, is his declaration that this will be done entirely on his terms.
