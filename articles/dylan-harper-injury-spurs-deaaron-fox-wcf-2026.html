Dylan Harper Hamstring Injury Update: Spurs Rookie Exits WCF Game 2, De'Aaron Fox Also Out — What We Know

Dylan Harper suffered a right hamstring injury in the third quarter of Game 2 against the Thunder. De'Aaron Fox missed his second consecutive game with a high ankle sprain. The Spurs lost 122–113 and face a crisis in their backcourt heading into Game 3.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 21, 2026·4 min read·👁 29,884
Share:

The San Antonio Spurs are in serious injury trouble. Rookie guard Dylan Harper left Game 2 of the Western Conference Finals in the third quarter with a right hamstring injury and did not return, delivering a devastating blow to a team already without All-Star guard De'Aaron Fox, who missed his second consecutive game with a right high ankle sprain. The Oklahoma City Thunder won Game 2 122–113 to level the series at 1–1. Harper will undergo an MRI on Thursday in San Antonio. Game 3 is Friday.

Spurs Injury Report — WCF Game 2
  • Dylan Harper: Right hamstring strain — exited Q3, MRI Thursday
  • De'Aaron Fox: Right high ankle sprain — missed Games 1 and 2, game-time decision for Game 3
  • Game 2 result: Thunder 122, Spurs 113 — series tied 1–1
  • Game 3: Friday, May 22 — at San Antonio
  • Stephon Castle turnovers: 20 in two games (playoff record per ESPN)
  • Harper's Game 1 stat line: 24 pts, 11 reb, 6 ast — historic rookie performance

How did Dylan Harper get hurt?

Harper took two awkward falls in quick succession during the third quarter of Game 2, the second occurring after colliding with Thunder star Shai Gilgeous-Alexander underneath the basket on a driving layup attempt. He clutched his right leg immediately, was helped to the Spurs bench, and was eventually ruled out for the remainder of the game. He was briefly seen moving around the bench area but spent most of the second half in the locker room receiving treatment.

The Spurs were deliberately vague about the injury after the game. Coach Mitch Johnson declined to specify beyond "leg injury," and the team's TV play-by-play announcer noted during the broadcast that the nature of the injury was unclear. Sources speaking to ESPN confirmed a hamstring strain and said Harper would have the MRI in San Antonio Thursday morning, with results expected to clarify whether he'll be available Friday.

How long is Dylan Harper out? What do the doctors say?

Harper's availability for Game 3 is genuinely uncertain. For a mild hamstring strain with 48 hours of rest, a return is possible — the NBA playoffs create enormous pressure on teams and players to push through soft-tissue injuries. For a moderate to severe strain, the timeline extends to 1–3 weeks, which would likely end Harper's playoff run. The MRI results are the key. A doctor speaking to Yahoo Sports estimated that if it is a Grade 1 strain (mild), Harper could play Friday with treatment; a Grade 2 strain (moderate) would put Game 3 in serious doubt and potentially end his series.

"This'll just be kind of the world we live in, regardless of whether Fox plays or not." — Spurs coach Mitch Johnson, on managing the injury situation

What about De'Aaron Fox? When is he coming back?

Fox suffered a right high ankle sprain in Game 4 of the Western Conference Semifinals against Minnesota and has not played since. He warmed up with the team ahead of both Game 1 and Game 2, but his ankle was not ready in either instance. Johnson has made clear Fox's return is a game-by-game decision — "status quo moving forward" — and that the Spurs will not force him back before the ankle is functional enough to play on. Fox is a game-time decision for Game 3 on Friday.

Can the Spurs survive without both guards?

It is a genuine crisis. With both Harper and Fox unavailable in Game 2, second-year guard Stephon Castle ran the offense — and was catastrophic with the ball. Castle committed 20 turnovers across the first two games, the most turnovers in any two-game span by any player in NBA playoff history according to ESPN Research. Those 20 turnovers led directly to 55 Thunder points. The Spurs' coaching staff has publicly expressed confidence in Castle, but the numbers are alarming. Victor Wembanyama's 34-point, 12-rebound effort in Game 2 kept the game competitive — but OKC's depth and the Spurs' backcourt problems are a serious structural mismatch heading into a must-win Game 3 in San Antonio.
