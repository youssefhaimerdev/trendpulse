Sam Merrill Game 7: The Performance That Made Him the Most-Searched Athlete in America

Sam Merrill scored 29 points on 13-of-17 shooting as the Cleveland Cavaliers eliminated the Detroit Pistons 118–95 in Game 7. One performance turned a little-known sharpshooter into a trending topic for over a million searches.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 18, 2026·4 min read·👁 91,447 views
Share:

The Cavaliers won Game 7. Cleveland defeated Detroit 118–95 on Sunday night to advance to the Eastern Conference Semifinals, and the story of the game — the reason over a million people searched his name — was reserve guard Sam Merrill. The 28-year-old from Logan, Utah, came off the bench and put on one of the most efficient shooting performances in recent playoff history: 29 points on 13-of-17 from the field, including 7-of-9 from three-point range.

Sam Merrill — Game 7 Stats
  • Points: 29
  • Field goals: 13-of-17 (76.5%)
  • Three-pointers: 7-of-9 (77.8%)
  • Minutes: 28
  • +/-: +24
  • Final score: Cavaliers 118, Pistons 95

Who is Sam Merrill?

Sam Merrill went undrafted in 2020 and spent time in the G League before carving out an NBA career as a specialist shooter. He played college ball at Utah State, where he became one of the program's all-time leading scorers. At 6-foot-5, he lacks the athleticism of most NBA wing players but compensates with elite shot mechanics and basketball IQ. His career three-point percentage entering this postseason was 39.4% — efficient, but not eye-catching. Sunday night was a different level entirely.

How the game unfolded

Cleveland controlled the game from the first quarter. Donovan Mitchell led all scorers with 31 points, but it was Merrill's scoring bursts that repeatedly pushed the lead from manageable to insurmountable every time Detroit tried to make a run. The most decisive sequence came in the third quarter, when Merrill hit back-to-back threes on consecutive possessions to extend a 9-point lead to 15. Detroit never recovered.

"Merrill was unconscious. When he gets going like that there's nothing you can do. Every shot was pure." — Cavaliers coach Kenny Atkinson

What it means for Cleveland

The Cavaliers advanced to the Eastern Conference Semifinals, where they would meet the New York Knicks — a series that began with one of the most dramatic comebacks in playoff history (New York's 22-point fourth-quarter deficit erased in Game 1). The Cavs came in as the higher seed and conference favorite, but Merrill's performance against Detroit showed exactly the kind of secondary scoring threat that makes Cleveland so difficult to gameplan against. When role players catch fire, this team becomes truly dangerous.
