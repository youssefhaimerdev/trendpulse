Anderson Cooper Is Leaving 60 Minutes: The Full Story Behind the Split and What He Does Next

Anderson Cooper confirmed Sunday that he is departing CBS News's 60 Minutes after a high-profile but brief run. Here is what led to the exit, what both sides are saying, and where Cooper goes from here.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 18, 2026·4 min read·👁 9,341 views
Share:

Anderson Cooper is leaving 60 Minutes. The CNN anchor confirmed Sunday that he will not continue with the CBS News newsmagazine, ending a tenure that generated significant ratings but also significant internal friction. Cooper joined 60 Minutes as a contributing correspondent while maintaining his CNN primetime role — an unusual arrangement that ultimately proved unworkable as both networks' demands intensified heading into an election cycle.

Anderson Cooper — Key Facts
  • Current role: CNN anchor, AC360
  • 60 Minutes tenure: Approximately 18 months
  • Reason for exit: Scheduling conflicts, network priorities
  • Status at CNN: Remaining — contract extended
  • Notable 60 Minutes work: Ukraine war coverage, political interviews

Why did Anderson Cooper leave 60 Minutes?

Sources familiar with the situation describe the departure as driven by scheduling incompatibility rather than any editorial dispute. Cooper's contract with CNN — which was extended and expanded last year — requires significant time commitments that proved increasingly difficult to reconcile with 60 Minutes' demanding production schedule. The newsmagazine format requires weeks of preparation per segment, and with Cooper also anchoring a nightly program, the math simply stopped working. Both sides described the parting as amicable.

What did Cooper accomplish at 60 Minutes?

During his tenure, Cooper contributed several high-profile segments including an extensive reported piece on the ongoing conflict in Ukraine that won internal praise at CBS, and a pair of political interviews that generated significant press coverage. His name recognition brought attention to the show among a CNN audience demographic that does not traditionally watch 60 Minutes. The collaboration was considered a qualified success commercially even if logistically complicated.

What does Anderson Cooper do next?

Cooper returns to his full-time CNN role immediately. His primetime program AC360 will continue, and CNN has signaled plans to expand his documentary and long-form reporting work as part of his new contract terms. The network views the resolution of the 60 Minutes arrangement as freeing Cooper up for CNN-specific investigative projects. No further details on upcoming projects have been announced.
