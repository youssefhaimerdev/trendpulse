Thunder Beat Spurs 127-114 in Game 5: SGA's 32 Points Put OKC One Win From the NBA Finals

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander scored 32 points — his best performance of the series — and the Oklahoma City Thunder beat the San Antonio Spurs 127-114 in Game 5 of the Western Conference Finals. The Thunder lead 3-2 and are one win from the NBA Finals. San Antonio faces elimination in Game 6 on Thursday.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 27, 2026·5 min read·👁 94,223
Share:

Oklahoma City won Game 5. The Thunder defeated the Spurs 127–114 Tuesday night at Paycom Center in Oklahoma City, taking a 3–2 series lead and putting San Antonio on the brink of elimination. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander authored his finest performance of the series with 32 points and 9 assists, and the Thunder's supporting cast was dominant in the paint. Game 6 is Thursday at 8:30 PM ET, back in San Antonio. The Spurs must win or go home.

Game 5 Final — May 27, 2026
  • Final: Thunder 127, Spurs 114
  • Series: Thunder lead 3-2
  • SGA: 32 pts, 9 ast — highest-scoring game of series
  • Alex Caruso: 22 pts, 4-of-8 from three

How Game 5 played out

Champagnie came out on fire for San Antonio, scoring 13 of the Spurs' first 18 points and briefly making Paycom Center nervous. But SGA — who was quiet and 0-for-4 from the field early — came alive at the free throw line to tie the game, and once he got going there was no stopping him. Dylan Harper, back from his hamstring scare, contributed a 5-0 run that stretched the OKC lead to 20 in the fourth quarter before the Spurs mounted a brief challenge.

The Spurs cut it to nine with under six minutes left on a Devin Vassell three — the kind of moment that made the crowd uneasy — but Jared McCain, inserted into the starting lineup by coach Mark Daigneault, knocked down a three to push the lead back to 12, and San Antonio never got closer. Alex Caruso's four-three performance (4-of-8 from deep) was the most efficient supporting act of the series for OKC, and the Thunder's interior dominance — Hartenstein's 15 rebounds plus Holmgren's 11 — neutralized whatever rhythm the Spurs tried to find around Wembanyama.

"We understand the moment. We've been here before. One more." — SGA, postgame

Victor Wembanyama's difficult night

This was not Wembanyama's night. He shot 4-of-15 from the field including 0-of-5 from three, finished with a playoff-low -8 plus/minus, and was not the best player on the court for the first time all series. The Thunder's defensive scheme — switching everything, forcing Wemby to initiate offense with his back to the basket rather than on the roll — frustrated him all night. He still had 20 points and three blocks, but that level of play is simply not enough to beat this Thunder team. The Spurs need a dominant Wembanyama in Game 6, and they need him immediately.

Spurs vs Thunder Game 6: where to watch, time, channel

Game 6 is Thursday, May 28 at 8:30 PM ET at the AT&T Center in San Antonio. The game will air on ESPN and is streamable on the ESPN app, Hulu + Live TV, Sling TV, and FuboTV. The Spurs are 73.1% likely to win and advance when hosting a Game 6 in a tied series historically — but this Thunder team has not shown much respect for historical odds. San Antonio needs a dominant home performance and a completely different version of Wembanyama. Everything is on the line.

Also: Knicks clinch first Finals berth since 1999

In the Eastern Conference, the New York Knicks clinched their spot in the NBA Finals earlier Tuesday, routing the Cleveland Cavaliers to win the ECF. It is New York's first Finals appearance since 1999. The Knicks will await the winner of Spurs-Thunder. A Thunder vs Knicks Finals would be one of the most anticipated NBA Championship matchups in years — two franchise resurgences, one stage.
