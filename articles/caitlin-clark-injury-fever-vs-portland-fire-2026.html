Why Caitlin Clark Didn't Play vs Portland Fire: Back Injury Explained and What Comes Next

Caitlin Clark was a surprise late scratch for the Indiana Fever on Wednesday with back soreness. The Fever won without her, 90–73. Here's what we know about the injury, whether it's serious, and when she's expected back.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 21, 2026·4 min read·👁 41,330
Share:

Caitlin Clark did not play Wednesday night. The Indiana Fever ruled their star guard out against the Portland Fire approximately 100 minutes before tip-off on May 20, listing her with a back injury. It was the first game Clark has missed in the 2026 WNBA season. Indiana won anyway, 90–73, with Tyasha Harris starting in Clark's place. Clark is considered day-to-day and is expected to return Friday against the Golden State Valkyries.

Caitlin Clark Injury — Key Facts
  • Injury: Back soreness / stiffness
  • Status: Day-to-day (per coach Stephanie White)
  • Game missed: Fever vs Portland Fire, May 20
  • Result without Clark: Fever won 90–73
  • Clark's 2026 stats: 24.3 pts, 9.8 ast, 5.0 reb per game
  • Next game: Friday, May 22 vs Golden State Valkyries
  • Is it serious? Not believed to be — described as precautionary

What exactly is Caitlin Clark's injury?

Clark did not participate in practice on Tuesday and instead received treatment. She woke up Wednesday feeling stiff in her back, and the Fever's medical staff made the call to hold her out. Head coach Stephanie White was careful not to call it a significant injury. "She woke up with some stiffness and some soreness," White said before the game. "It's not the time to take a chance. We just really want to be cautious."

This is not Clark's first back issue this season. In Indiana's very first game of 2026, Clark left the bench multiple times to get back adjustments. After that game, she told reporters: "Just getting my back adjusted. It gets out of line pretty quickly — but I feel great." Wednesday's scratch appears to be a more acute version of that same ongoing management process.

Initial reports from beat reporter Scott Agness suggested the absence was part of a "strategic management plan" given the Fever's demanding schedule — four games in eight days. White pushed back on that framing post-game, insisting Clark is not being load-managed: "She's healthy. We're not managing anything. This is just a back issue we want to give time to be ready."

Why are Fever fans so worried?

The concern among Indiana's fanbase is understandable in context. In the 2025 WNBA season, Clark played just 13 games before a groin injury ended her year entirely, costing her the All-Star game in her home city and the Fever's playoff run. Any injury news this season triggers immediate anxiety — and rightfully so, given how much the WNBA's popularity depends on Clark being on the floor.

The reassuring detail here is the nature of the injury. A back that gets stiff and needs management is a chronic maintenance issue — not a structural injury. The Fever have been cautious with their players all season, and White's post-game tone suggested strongly that this is not a situation requiring any prolonged absence.

"I'm not a doctor, so I don't know. But I don't anticipate this being an ongoing issue." — Stephanie White, Fever head coach

How did the Fever do without Caitlin Clark?

Indiana handled business. Tyasha Harris started at guard and distributed the ball effectively, finishing with 14 points and 7 assists. Aliyah Boston, returning from her own injury absence, added 16 points and 8 rebounds. The Fever's defense was excellent — holding Portland to under 40% shooting — and the win moved Indiana to 3-2 on the young season.

Is Caitlin Clark playing Friday vs Golden State?

Clark is expected to play Friday, though the Fever have not officially confirmed her status. White said she does not anticipate the back issue to be an ongoing problem and that Clark will be evaluated day-by-day leading into the game. The Golden State Valkyries game is scheduled for 7:00 PM ET on Friday, May 22 and will air on ESPN2. Check Indiana's official injury report — which the WNBA requires teams to update by the day before each game — for the official update.
