Knicks vs Cavaliers Game 1 Final Score: Brunson's 38 Points Fuel One of the Greatest Comebacks in NBA Playoff History

The New York Knicks won Game 1 of the Eastern Conference Finals 115–104. They trailed by 22 points with under 8 minutes left. Then Jalen Brunson happened.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 20, 2026·Updated 2h ago·5 min read·👁 91,447 views
Share:

Yes, the Knicks won. New York defeated the Cleveland Cavaliers 115–104 in overtime on Tuesday night at Madison Square Garden to take a 1–0 lead in the 2026 Eastern Conference Finals. The result looked impossible with 7 minutes and 52 seconds left in regulation, when the Cavs led by 22 points and ESPN Analytics put Cleveland's win probability at 99.9%. What followed was a 44–11 Knicks run across the final minutes of regulation and all of overtime — one of the greatest comebacks in NBA playoff history.

Game 1 Quick Facts — May 19, 2026
  • Final score: New York Knicks 115, Cleveland Cavaliers 104 (OT)
  • Venue: Madison Square Garden, New York City
  • Jalen Brunson: 38 points, 6 assists, 5 rebounds
  • Knicks' largest deficit: 22 points in Q4
  • Cleveland's win probability at 7:52 of Q4: 99.9%
  • Knicks run: 44–11 over final 7:40 of Q4 + full OT
  • Cavs OT scoring: 3 points total
  • Series: Knicks lead 1–0 (best of 7)

What happened? The full Game 1 breakdown

For the first three and a half quarters, this looked like Cleveland's game to lose — and then they lost it. The Cavaliers built a 22-point lead on the strength of strong ball movement, a Knicks offense shooting just 11% from three at halftime, and consistent pressure on Jalen Brunson defensively. When Cleveland pushed the lead to 93–71 with 7:52 left in the fourth quarter, the Knicks looked beaten.

Then Knicks coach Mike Brown made a tactical decision that changed everything. Seeing the Cavaliers exploit Brunson on defense, Brown turned the tables — sending Brunson directly at James Harden on the other end. The veteran Harden, now in the back end of his career, could not keep up. Brunson ran him ragged, scoring in bunches on mismatches and drawing fouls while the Knicks' three-point shooting suddenly found its range.

"You got to do what the game dictates. They were doing the same thing with Jalen. So we said two can play that game." — Knicks coach Mike Brown

The Cavaliers, meanwhile, completely unraveled. Cleveland committed eight turnovers in the last 13 minutes of the fourth quarter and overtime. The team that had looked composed and dominant for three quarters simply fell apart — unable to execute, unable to score, unable to stop New York's momentum.

Jalen Brunson stats: The performance that will be talked about for years

Brunson finished with 38 points, 6 assists, and 5 rebounds in 43 minutes. His fourth quarter and overtime were the stuff of legend. He attacked mismatches relentlessly, got to the free throw line repeatedly, and made the big shots when the Knicks could least afford a miss. His ability to force the Cavaliers into the Harden mismatch was the tactical chess move of the game — and he exploited it mercilessly.

Other key Knicks contributors included OG Anunoby, who hit crucial free throws in overtime, and Landry Shamet, who buried a 25-foot three with under two minutes left in OT to extend the lead to nine and effectively ice the game. Mikal Bridges also came up with a pivotal steal in overtime when the Cavs desperately needed a stop.

Cleveland's collapse: What went wrong for the Cavaliers

For Cleveland, the game will haunt this series. Donovan Mitchell was excellent in the first half but the Cavs' offensive identity broke down in the final minutes. Evan Mobley missed a three-point attempt in the closing seconds of overtime. Max Strus hit a timely elbow three late in OT, but it was too little too late. The Cavaliers scored just 3 points in the entire overtime period.

The Harden experiment — a calculated decision to use Harden's defensive limitations as a weapon — backfired catastrophically once the Knicks identified it. The Cavs could not adjust. Head coach Kenny Atkinson will need answers before Game 2.

What channel is Knicks vs Cavaliers Game 2 on? Where to watch

Game 2 of the Eastern Conference Finals is scheduled for Thursday, May 21, with tip-off at 8:00 PM ET. The game will air on ESPN and stream on the ESPN app. You can also watch via NBA League Pass (out-of-market subscribers) or catch it on Sling TV, FuboTV, DirecTV Stream, and Hulu + Live TV if you have access to ESPN through those services.

Series outlook: History favors the Knicks

The team that wins Game 1 of the Conference Finals has gone on to win the series 78.2% of the time in NBA history. That's a stark number for Cleveland to wake up to. The Knicks now have home-court advantage and the psychological boost of the comeback. The Cavaliers, despite the loss, proved for three and a half quarters they can control this series — the question is whether they can sustain 48 full minutes rather than just 36.

The stakes are high on both sides. The Knicks are chasing their first NBA Finals appearance since 1999. Cleveland, under the continued leadership of Donovan Mitchell, are trying to prove this core is ready for a championship run. After Tuesday night, New York has the momentum — and Madison Square Garden behind them.
