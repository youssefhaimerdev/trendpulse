Blue Moon Tonight, May 30: What Time to Look Up, What It Is, and Why This One Is Smaller Than Usual

Tonight's full moon is a Blue Moon — the second full moon of May 2026. It peaks at 4:45 AM ET Sunday morning but the best time to see it is Saturday evening at moonrise. It's also a micromoon, the smallest full moon of the year.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 30, 2026·3 min read·👁 62,118
Share:

Yes, it is a full moon tonight. Saturday evening, May 30, 2026, the full moon rises in the eastern sky after sunset — and because it is the second full moon of May, it qualifies as a Blue Moon. The exact peak of illumination comes at 4:45 AM ET Sunday morning (8:45 UTC May 31), but the best viewing for most people in the Americas is Saturday evening when the moon rises dramatically against the darkening sky. This moon comes with a bonus twist: it is also a micromoon — the farthest, smallest full moon of 2026.

Blue Moon May 2026 — Key Facts
  • Type: Blue Moon (second full moon in May) + Micromoon
  • Peak illumination: 4:45 AM ET Sunday, May 31 (8:45 UTC)
  • Best viewing: Saturday evening, May 30 — look east after sunset
  • Will it look blue? No — expect gold, orange, or amber near horizon; silver overhead
  • Constellation nearby: Antares (red star, Heart of Scorpius)
  • Why "micromoon": Moon is at its farthest point from Earth — appears ~7% smaller than average
  • May's first full moon: May 1 (the Flower Moon)
  • Next full moon: June (the Strawberry Moon)

What is a Blue Moon?

A Blue Moon has nothing to do with color. The most common modern definition is simply the second full moon in a single calendar month. Since May 2026 had a full moon on May 1 (the Flower Moon) and has another tonight, tonight's moon gets the Blue Moon label. These happen roughly once every 2-3 years — hence the expression "once in a blue moon" to describe something rare. There is also a seasonal definition (the third full moon in a season that has four), but the monthly definition is what most people mean when they use the phrase today.

Why is this a micromoon? What does that mean?

The Moon doesn't orbit Earth in a perfect circle — it follows an elliptical path, meaning there are times when it's closer to Earth (perigee) and times when it's farther away (apogee). A micromoon occurs when a full moon coincides with the Moon being near apogee — its farthest point. Tonight's Blue Moon is the most distant full moon of 2026, sitting about 252,000 miles from Earth. It will appear roughly 7% smaller and 25-30% dimmer than a supermoon (which occurs when the full moon is closest). Most people will not notice the difference with the naked eye.

Will the moon actually look blue?

No. Blue Moons are almost never visually blue. When the full moon rises tonight, it will likely appear orange or amber near the horizon — the same warm hue caused by light scattering through Earth's thick atmosphere at low angles. As it climbs higher in the sky, it will fade to its usual silver-white. The only time moons genuinely appear blue is after certain volcanic eruptions or major wildfires that release fine particles into the atmosphere — there is no such event affecting tonight's sky.

What else is visible near the moon tonight?

Sky watchers in the eastern US and elsewhere should look for the bright reddish star Antares glowing close to the lower left of the moon. Antares is the heart of the constellation Scorpius — one of the most easily identified summer constellations — and its red-orange color makes for a beautiful contrast against the rising full moon. No telescope or binoculars needed. Just a clear eastern horizon and clear skies.

Best places to watch the moonrise tonight

For the most dramatic moonrise view, find a location with a clear, unobstructed eastern horizon. A beach, open hilltop, lake shore, rooftop, or park free of tall buildings will give you the best view of the moon as it climbs above the horizon in the blue hour just after sunset. The moon rises at slightly different times depending on your city — use timeanddate.com/moon to find your exact local moonrise time.
