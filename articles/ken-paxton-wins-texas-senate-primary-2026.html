Ken Paxton Defeats John Cornyn in Texas Senate Runoff: What Trump's Win Means for November

Ken Paxton ended John Cornyn's 24-year Senate career Tuesday, winning the Texas Republican primary runoff after a last-minute Trump endorsement. Paxton now faces Democrat James Talarico in what could be one of the most expensive Senate races of 2026.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 27, 2026·5 min read·👁 38,774
Share:

Ken Paxton won. Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton defeated incumbent Senator John Cornyn in the Republican primary runoff for the U.S. Senate on Tuesday, May 26, 2026. The Associated Press called the race shortly after 8 PM CT — roughly one hour after most Texas polls closed. The result ends Cornyn's 24-year career in the Senate and sets up a potentially explosive general election matchup between Paxton and Democratic nominee James Talarico in November.

Texas Senate Runoff Results — May 26, 2026
  • Winner: Ken Paxton (R) — advancing to November general election
  • Defeated: John Cornyn — incumbent, first elected to Senate in 2002
  • Trump endorsement: May 19, 2026 — one week before the election
  • First-round results (March 3): Cornyn 42%, Paxton 41%, Wesley Hunt 13.5%
  • Democratic nominee: James Talarico — won primary over Jasmine Crockett (53-45.7%)
  • General election: November 2026
  • Paxton's background: Texas AG since 2014; led 2020 election lawsuit; survived 2023 impeachment

What decided the race: Trump's endorsement

For months the Paxton-Cornyn race looked too close to call. Cornyn had the institutional Republican backing, the campaign war chest, and the active support of Senate Majority Leader John Thune and most of his Senate colleagues. Paxton had the grassroots MAGA energy and the polling advantage among Republican primary voters who had moved decisively toward Trump loyalism since 2022. Neither had a commanding lead.

Then, seven days before the election, President Donald Trump endorsed Paxton. In his endorsement statement, Trump said Cornyn "was not supportive of me when times were tough," pointing specifically to Cornyn's neutrality in the 2016 primary, his 2023 comment that Trump's "time has passed him by," and his description of criminal charges against Trump as "very serious." Polling already showed Paxton ahead — but Trump's endorsement transformed a competitive race into a decisive one.

"Texas is not for sale. The people always win." — Ken Paxton, election night

What it means: The end of the Republican establishment in Texas

Cornyn had been the most powerful Republican in Texas for a generation. He served as Senate Majority Whip, built deep relationships across Washington, and was considered one of the most effective legislative operators in the Senate. His loss is not just personal — it is a signal about the state of the Republican Party in 2026. Trump's ability to end the career of a 24-year Senate incumbent by endorsing his challenger one week before the election will reverberate far beyond Texas.

The Texas Tribune called the result "a watershed moment for GOP politics in Texas" and noted that it "cements the president's role in shaping the Republican Party's nominees for the 2026 midterm elections." For Republicans who have not sufficiently demonstrated loyalty to Trump, the message from Tuesday night is unmistakable.

Who is James Talarico? The November matchup

Paxton now faces James Talarico, a 36-year-old Austin-area state representative and former San Antonio public school teacher who defeated Congresswoman Jasmine Crockett in the Democratic primary. Talarico launched his Senate campaign in September 2025 and has positioned himself as a working-class economic populist — specifically targeting the wealth gap between billionaires and struggling Texans.

Democrats made no secret of the fact they preferred to face Paxton over Cornyn. Talarico called Paxton "corrupt" on election night and framed the fall race as "the people versus Ken Paxton." Paxton carries substantial baggage: he was impeached by the Texas House in 2023 on charges including bribery, abuse of office, and obstruction before the Republican-controlled Senate acquitted him on all counts. He also led the 2020 election lawsuit attempting to overturn results in four states — which the Supreme Court rejected without hearing.

Texas has not elected a Democratic Senator since 1988. But Talarico is expected to be the best-funded Democratic challenger the state has seen in decades, and national Democrats see Paxton's legal history as genuine vulnerability in a state where the electorate is shifting. The November race will almost certainly be one of the most expensive Senate contests of the 2026 cycle.
