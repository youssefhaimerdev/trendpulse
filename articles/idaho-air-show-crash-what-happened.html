Idaho Air Show Crash: What Happened, What We Know About Casualties, and the Latest on the Investigation

A plane crash during an air show in Idaho sent emergency crews scrambling and prompted a federal investigation. Here is a full breakdown of what happened and the latest confirmed information.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 18, 2026·3 min read·👁 18,773 views
Share:

A aircraft performing at an air show in Idaho crashed during a demonstration flight on Sunday, injuring multiple people and prompting an immediate response from local emergency services, the Idaho State Police, and the Federal Aviation Administration. The crash occurred in front of a crowd of spectators, several of whom were treated at the scene for injuries described as ranging from minor to serious. The FAA has opened a formal investigation.

  • Where: Idaho (specific venue withheld pending next of kin notification)
  • Injuries: Multiple — ranging from minor to serious
  • Investigating agency: FAA + NTSB (National Transportation Safety Board)
  • Aircraft type: Not yet officially confirmed

What we know about the crash

Witnesses at the event reported that the aircraft appeared to experience a mechanical issue during a low-altitude maneuver before losing control and impacting the ground. Emergency services arrived within minutes and the air show was immediately halted. The pilot's condition has not been officially confirmed as of the time of publication. The FAA has dispatched investigators to the scene, and the NTSB is expected to open a parallel investigation given the public setting of the incident.

Air show crashes: how common are they?

Air show accidents, while rare relative to the total number of performances annually, do occur. The FAA monitors all air show performers and requires certification for aerobatic maneuvers at public events. The NTSB maintains a database of air show incidents and typically completes full investigations within 12 to 18 months of an accident. Final reports, including probable cause, are published publicly. Given the number of air shows held in the United States each year — approximately 300 major events — fatal incidents are statistically infrequent but draw significant public attention when they do happen.
