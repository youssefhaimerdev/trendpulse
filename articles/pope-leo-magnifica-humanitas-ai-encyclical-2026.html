Pope Leo XIV's Magnifica Humanitas Explained: What the AI Encyclical Says and Why Anthropic's Co-Founder Spoke at the Vatican

Pope Leo XIV released his first encyclical on May 25, 2026 — a 235-page document on artificial intelligence titled Magnifica Humanitas. In an unprecedented move, he presented it personally alongside Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah. Here is what the document says and why this moment matters.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 25, 2026·5 min read·👁 14,229
Share:

Pope Leo XIV made history on Monday in two distinct ways. He became the first pope in history to personally present an encyclical to the world — breaking a tradition where such presentations are delegated to cardinals — and he did so standing alongside Christopher Olah, the 33-year-old co-founder of AI company Anthropic. The document, Magnifica Humanitas: On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence, is the most significant religious statement on AI ever produced and immediately became one of the most widely discussed documents of 2026.

Magnifica Humanitas — Key Facts
  • Full title: Magnifica Humanitas: On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence
  • Released: May 25, 2026 at the Vatican's Synod Hall
  • Signed: May 15, 2026 — 135th anniversary of Pope Leo XIII's Rerum Novarum
  • Length: 235 pages
  • Anthropic speaker: Christopher Olah, co-founder and head of interpretability research
  • Pope's position: Neither utopian nor apocalyptic about AI — calls for safeguards and external regulation
  • Key warning: Concentration of AI power in few hands is a "danger" to the vulnerable

What does Magnifica Humanitas actually say?

The encyclical is 235 pages and draws on Catholic social teaching traditions going back to Leo XIII's 1891 Rerum Novarum — the landmark document on labor and capitalism that established the Church's framework for engaging with economic and technological transformation. Leo XIV's document applies that same framework to artificial intelligence.

The Pope's position avoids both extremes. He neither dismisses AI as a passing threat nor embraces a techno-utopian vision. Instead, Magnifica Humanitas calls for robust external regulation of AI development, with particular concern for three areas: the use of AI in warfare (which Leo warns risks removing human conscience from lethal decisions), the concentration of AI data and power in the hands of a small number of private companies, and the vulnerability of children and economically marginalized communities to harm from unregulated AI systems.

The document explicitly states that "the gains of AI must be shared globally," not concentrated in wealthy nations and corporations — a direct challenge to the current architecture of the AI industry.

Why was Anthropic's Chris Olah at the Vatican?

Olah's presence was the detail that made this story extraordinary. He is an atheist AI researcher who co-founded Anthropic specifically because of his concern about AI safety — the company's mission is explicitly to build AI systems that are safe and understandable. The Vatican chose him because of that alignment in values, not despite his lack of faith.

In his remarks to the packed Vatican auditorium — seated in a row alongside cardinals and theologians — Olah spoke directly about the limitations of AI companies' internal accountability: "Every frontier AI lab, including Anthropic, operates inside a set of incentives and constraints that can sometimes conflict with doing the right thing. That is why it is enormously important that there be people outside those incentives — people who care about things going well and insist on safety, who are willing to say hard things."

He described Magnifica Humanitas as an example of exactly that external voice: "That is what I see in Magnifica Humanitas, and it is why I am grateful to His Holiness and to the Church for taking up this work of discernment."

"We dwell so often on what divides us, but humanity, full of dignity and conscience, has so much common ground." — Christopher Olah, Anthropic co-founder, speaking at the Vatican

The political context: Anthropic vs. the Trump administration

Olah's Vatican appearance carries political weight beyond theology. In February 2026, the Trump administration ordered all US federal agencies to stop using Anthropic's AI technology and imposed penalties on the company for refusing to allow the US military unrestricted access to its AI systems. Anthropic is currently suing the administration, accusing it of illegal retaliation. The White House has not commented on Olah's Vatican appearance.

The timing — Anthropic's co-founder appearing alongside the Pope to argue that AI development should be subject to external ethical oversight — reads, in that context, as a deliberate statement about what kind of oversight the company welcomes. Leo's encyclical, which calls for governments and international bodies to regulate AI rather than leaving it to self-policing by companies, aligns closely with Anthropic's public position on safety regulation.

Why does this encyclical matter beyond the Vatican?

Encyclicals carry weight beyond the 1.3 billion Catholics worldwide who may be directly addressed by them. They function as formal intellectual contributions to global policy debates — the kind of document that governments, international organizations, and academic institutions engage with seriously. Leo's choice to make AI the subject of his first encyclical, to sign it on the anniversary of Rerum Novarum, and to personally present it alongside one of the AI industry's most prominent safety advocates signals that the Catholic Church intends to be a major voice in the coming decade of AI governance debates. That is genuinely significant regardless of one's religious views.
