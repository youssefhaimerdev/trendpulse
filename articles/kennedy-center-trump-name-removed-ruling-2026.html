Judge Orders Trump's Name Removed from Kennedy Center and Blocks Planned 2-Year Closure

Federal Judge Christopher Cooper issued a sweeping 94-page ruling Friday that Trump's board illegally renamed the Kennedy Center and that only Congress can change a name Congress created. All signage must come down within 14 days.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 30, 2026·4 min read·👁 31,774
Share:

A federal judge on Friday ordered all signage bearing President Trump's name removed from the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts within 14 days and temporarily blocked the administration's plan to close the cultural landmark for a two-year renovation. U.S. District Court Judge Christopher Cooper — issuing a 94-page ruling on what would have been President Kennedy's birthday — found that the Kennedy Center's board had acted illegally when it renamed the venue "the Donald J. Trump and John F. Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts" in December 2025 without Congressional authorization.

Kennedy Center Ruling — Key Points
  • Judge: Christopher Cooper, U.S. District Court for D.C.
  • Ruling issued: Friday, May 29, 2026 — on JFK's birthday
  • Ruling length: 94 pages
  • Key finding: "Congress gave the Kennedy Center its name, and only Congress can change it"
  • Name removal deadline: All signage and online materials within 14 days
  • Closure blocked: Planned 2-year closure starting July 4 is on hold
  • Board voting rights: Rep. Joyce Beatty's ex officio voting rights restored
  • Trump renamed it: December 2025, to "Donald J. Trump and John F. Kennedy Memorial Center"
  • Trump's response: Said judge "should be ashamed," announced he's handing control back to Congress

What the judge actually ruled — the legal reasoning

Judge Cooper's central argument is straightforward: Congress established the Kennedy Center in 1958 specifically to honor President Kennedy, and only Congress has the authority to change that mandate. The board Trump appointed cannot override a Congressional statute by majority vote in a board room meeting. "The Kennedy Center must be named for, and is meant to honor, President Kennedy alone," Cooper wrote. "Nor can any other individual be memorialized on the front portico of the building."

The ruling also found that the board's March vote to close the Center for two years was "ill-informed and seemingly preordained" — the result of a process that ignored the full range of the Center's obligations, particularly its role as a living memorial and active performance venue. The judge found the board "based its decision on an insufficient, one-sided presentation of information" and that it had failed to consider the damage closure would cause to programming, staff, and the institution's memorial mission.

"Congress gave the Kennedy Center its name, and only Congress can change it." — Judge Christopher Cooper, 94-page ruling

Trump's response: "Washing his hands" of the Kennedy Center

Trump reacted on Truth Social within hours of the ruling, calling the judge someone who "should be ashamed of himself" and suggesting the decision was politically motivated. In a notable shift, he then appeared to step back from the entire renovation and renaming effort, writing that he had "no interest in continuing what could only be a hopeless journey into 'NEVER NEVER LAND'" unless he was free to do things his way. He announced he would move to transfer control of the Kennedy Center back to Congress.

The White House separately noted this ruling was one of two significant legal setbacks in a single Friday — earlier in the day, a different federal judge had blocked the administration's $1.776 billion anti-weaponization compensation fund, which had been criticized by members of both parties as a political slush fund.

Who brought the case and what happens next

The ruling came in response to a lawsuit filed by Rep. Joyce Beatty, an Ohio Democrat who serves as an ex officio member of the Kennedy Center board. Beatty had argued that the board voted to strip her of her voting rights at a March 16 meeting where it approved both the name change and the closure plans. Judge Cooper agreed — restoring her voting rights and calling the board's actions an overreach of its authority.

The administration is expected to appeal. In the meantime, the 14-day deadline for physical signage removal is active — meaning workers must begin taking down "Trump Kennedy Center" signs, removing website references, and updating official documents within two weeks. Whether the administration complies or seeks a stay of that order pending appeal is the next development to watch.
