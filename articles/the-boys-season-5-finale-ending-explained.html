The Boys Season 5 Finale Ending Explained: Does Homelander Die? Who Lives, Who Dies, and How It All Ends

The Boys is over. The series finale "Blood and Bone" aired May 20, 2026 on Amazon Prime Video — and it answered every question the show spent five seasons building toward. Here is the complete breakdown, with full spoilers.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 20, 2026·Updated 1h ago·6 min read·👁 58,341 views
Share:
⚠ FULL SPOILERS AHEAD — This article explains the complete ending of The Boys season 5 finale, including all deaths, survivors, and the final scene. Read at your own risk.

Yes — Homelander dies in The Boys season 5 finale. The show's primary villain is killed in the Oval Office in a confrontation that involves Kimiko, Butcher, and Ryan before Billy Butcher deals the killing blow with his crowbar. But Homelander's death is far from the most gut-wrenching moment in the episode. The finale, titled "Blood and Bone", premiered on Amazon Prime Video on May 20, 2026 — and it does not give its characters a clean or comfortable ending.

The Boys Finale — Key Deaths & Fates at a Glance
  • Homelander: Dead — depowered by Kimiko, killed by Butcher in the Oval Office
  • Billy Butcher: Dead — shot and killed by Hughie at Vought Tower
  • The Deep: Dead — thrown into an ocean of predators by Starlight
  • Frenchie: Dead — died earlier in season, his sacrifice enables Kimiko's power
  • Terror (Butcher's dog): Dead — died of old age
  • Hughie Campbell: Alive — expecting a child with Annie/Starlight
  • Annie January (Starlight): Alive — pregnant, rebuilding
  • Mother's Milk (MM): Alive — gets married, reunites with family
  • Kimiko: Alive — moves to France
  • Ryan: Alive — distances himself from the violence

How does Homelander die? The Oval Office confrontation

The path to Homelander's death begins with Frenchie's sacrifice. Earlier in the season, Frenchie and Sister Sage successfully recreated the experiment that gave Soldier Boy a radiation-style superpower in the 1950s — and Frenchie transferred those abilities to Kimiko before his death. The power works like a nuclear blast that temporarily strips a Supe's abilities.

When Homelander — now granted V1 immortality by Soldier Boy and having assassinated the president — delivers an unhinged address from the Oval Office declaring himself a god, Butcher and the team move in. Ryan arrives to help, telling his father directly that no amount of power would stop him from ending up alone. The emotional weight cracks Homelander's composure.

Kimiko unleashes her Soldier Boy-derived blast, stripping Homelander of all his powers. What follows is the ultimate humiliation creator Eric Kripke had been building toward for five seasons: a powerless Homelander desperately trying to use his heat vision, attempting to fly away like "a kid pretending on a playground," and finally begging for his life. Butcher impales his crowbar into Homelander's skull and scalps him. The deed is broadcast to the world.

"I am the Homelander." — "No, you ain't nothing. And this is for my Becca." — Homelander's final exchange with Billy Butcher

Does Billy Butcher die? What happens to Butcher after Homelander

Killing Homelander does not end Butcher's arc — it begins his darkest chapter. With the Godolkin Virus in hand and Homelander dead, Butcher decides to use the Supe-killing virus to commit genocide — to wipe out all Supes entirely, rather than just the one he came for. He heads to Vought Tower.

Hughie confronts him, unable to let his friend cross that final line. A fight breaks out — and it ends with Hughie shooting Butcher. Billy Butcher dies in Hughie's arms, a regretful death that finally closes the loop on five seasons of self-destruction. Ryan, who had remained close to Butcher through the season, chooses to move on rather than stay attached to the violence.

What happens to every character — full survivor guide

Hughie Campbell and Annie January (Starlight) get the show's most hopeful ending. The couple are expecting a child together, and the finale closes on them looking toward a genuinely uncertain but possible future. Given everything the show put them through, it is the closest thing to a reward the series is willing to offer.

Mother's Milk retires from the fight and gets married, finally able to return to the family life the show repeatedly pulled him away from. His arc across five seasons was always about the cost of choosing the mission over the people you love — and the finale allows him to choose differently.

Kimiko moves to France, finally stepping away from the violence. The loss of Frenchie hangs over her ending, but she carries his memory — and the power he gave her — into a quieter life.

The Deep is killed by Starlight, who throws him into an ocean of predators. It is a fittingly absurd and brutal exit for one of the show's most consistently reviled characters. Sister Sage is also dispatched in the finale's closing acts.

Ryan chooses to walk away from both Homelander's shadow and Butcher's destructive path. The finale makes clear he could have gone either direction — and choosing to leave is framed as the bravest thing he does.

What does the ending mean? The show's final message

The Boys spent five seasons arguing that power corrupts and that institutions cannot be trusted to police themselves. The finale does not contradict that thesis — the corporate machine of Vought still exists, Stan Edgar is still alive somewhere, and the show ends without delivering systemic change. What changes are the individuals. Homelander is dead. Butcher is dead. The people who survived chose something different.

Creator Eric Kripke has said the show was always meant to end as a tragedy with "tiny threads of hope." That is exactly what "Blood and Bone" delivers — loss everywhere you look, and two people expecting a baby in spite of all of it. Whether that qualifies as hope or irony depends entirely on how you feel about the world the show was satirizing. Given the state of things, probably both.
