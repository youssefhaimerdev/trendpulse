Dodgers 4, Phillies 2: Wrobleski Takes No-Hitter Into 6th, Ohtani Homers as LA Wins Sixth Straight

Justin Wrobleski was nearly perfect for seven innings — career-high 9 strikeouts, no walks, and a no-hitter broken up by Kyle Schwarber's 411-foot blast in the 6th. Four solo home runs powered the Dodgers' sixth consecutive win.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 30, 2026·4 min read·👁 48,773
Share:

The Los Angeles Dodgers beat the Philadelphia Phillies 4-2 on Friday night at Dodger Stadium, extending their winning streak to a season-best six games behind a dominant performance from starting pitcher Justin Wrobleski. The 25-year-old left-hander took a no-hitter into the sixth inning, struck out a career-high nine batters without a walk, and was the story of the night in a matchup of two October contenders replaying their 2025 NLDS rivalry. Shohei Ohtani, Freddie Freeman, Max Muncy, and Will Smith each hit solo home runs off Phillies ace Zack Wheeler.

Dodgers 4, Phillies 2 — Game Summary
  • Final: Dodgers 4, Phillies 2
  • Winning pitcher: Justin Wrobleski (7-2, 3.07 ERA)
  • Losing pitcher: Zack Wheeler (4-1)
  • Save: Tanner Scott (5th)
  • Wrobleski: 7 IP, 1 H, 1 R, 0 BB, 9 K — career-high strikeouts
  • Dodgers HR: Ohtani (10th), Freeman (8th), Muncy (13th), W. Smith (6th)
  • Phillies HR: Schwarber (22nd) — broke up the no-hitter in 6th
  • Broadcast: Apple TV+
  • Dodgers record: 36-20 (NL West leaders, 6-game win streak)
  • Saturday: Luzardo (PHI) vs Roki Sasaki (LAD)

Wrobleski's near no-hitter — what happened pitch by pitch

Wrobleski was outstanding from the first pitch. He struck out the side in the first inning — Kyle Schwarber, Trea Turner, and Bryce Harper, all whiffing on a slider-heavy mix — and the tone was set immediately. He retired the first 11 Phillies he faced in order before Kyle Tucker dropped a fly ball in right field in the fourth inning to give Alec Bohm an unearned first base. The dropped ball technically put a runner on but didn't go into the scorebook as a hit — Wrobleski's no-hitter remained intact through five.

In the sixth with two outs, Schwarber launched a 411-foot bomb to dead center — his 22nd home run of the season — to break up both the no-hitter and the shutout simultaneously. The Citizen's Bank Park transplant didn't flinch. Wrobleski got Trea Turner to ground out to end the inning and never let the moment rattle him. He finished through seven innings on 91 pitches, allowing one hit, one run, zero walks, and nine strikeouts. The Dodgers' career strikeout record for Wrobleski. The best start of his career.

"I knew the no-hitter was there. I was trying not to think about it. Schwarber just got me." — Justin Wrobleski, postgame

Four solo home runs — the Dodgers' power display

While Wrobleski was the story on the mound, the Dodgers' offense did exactly what this team does: hit home runs. Freddie Freeman hit an opposite-field shot on the first pitch from Wheeler in the second inning. Max Muncy added one in the third. Shohei Ohtani — who entered the night with a .303 average, .548 slugging percentage, and 9 home runs — went deep in the fifth for his 10th. Will Smith added an insurance run in the seventh.

The four-homer game tied a career high for Wheeler in home runs allowed, which says more about the quality of the Dodgers lineup than anything else. Wheeler entered the night having allowed just one home run in his previous 37.2 innings. The Dodgers hit four in six innings.

What this means for both teams

For the Dodgers (36-20), this win extends their lead in the NL West to 4.5 games and continues the streak. The team looks like what everyone expected going into the season — a juggernaut. Wrobleski emerging as a genuine No. 2 starter behind whoever pitches Game 1 of the playoffs gives the rotation real depth.

For the Phillies (29-27), the loss was the third in a row and continues a concerning slide. Zack Wheeler's line — 6 IP, 4 ER, 4 HR allowed — will be an outlier in his otherwise exceptional season (1.67 ERA entering the night). The bigger issue is the lineup's struggles against left-handed starters, which has been a pattern all season. Saturday's game features Jesús Luzardo (PHI) vs Roki Sasaki (LAD) at 7:15 PM ET on Apple TV+.
