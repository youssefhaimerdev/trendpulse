Golden Knights Sweep Avalanche 4-0 to Reach the Stanley Cup Final: How Vegas Ended Colorado's Presidents' Trophy Dream

The Vegas Golden Knights swept the Presidents' Trophy-winning Colorado Avalanche in four straight games to advance to the 2026 Stanley Cup Final — their third Cup Final in franchise history. Mark Stone scored the series-clinching goal. Carter Hart was immovable in net.

By Daily Pulse Staff·May 27, 2026·4 min read·👁 44,119
Share:

The Vegas Golden Knights are going to the Stanley Cup Final. VGK defeated the Colorado Avalanche 2–1 in Game 4 on Tuesday night at T-Mobile Arena to complete a stunning 4-game sweep of the Presidents' Trophy winners. Mark Stone opened the scoring just 4:42 into the first period and Cole Smith added an insurance goal. Carter Hart made 20 saves to preserve the win. Colorado — the league's best regular season team — was swept without a single series win. Vegas advances to the Stanley Cup Final for the third time in franchise history.

Golden Knights Sweep — 2026 WCF
  • Series result: Golden Knights 4-0 (sweep)
  • Game 4 score: VGK 2, COL 1
  • Mark Stone: Scored in Games 3 and 4 — returned from lower-body injury
  • Carter Hart: 20 saves in Game 4 — 36 saves in Game 1
  • Gabriel Landeskog (COL): Scored Colorado's only goal in Game 4
  • Mackenzie Blackwood (COL): 24 saves in first start of series
  • Cale Makar (COL): Returned from injury, but Avs couldn't get rolling
  • Stanley Cup Final opponent: Hurricanes or Canadiens (CAR leads 2-1)
  • Last VGK Cup Final: 2023 (won the Cup)

How the Golden Knights swept the Presidents' Trophy winners

Colorado entered the series as the league's best team — 55-16-11, Presidents' Trophy winners, Cale Makar-led defensive corps, Valeri Nichushkin healthy for the playoffs. None of it mattered. Vegas was faster to pucks, harder to play against physically, and Carter Hart was outstanding in goal throughout the series.

In Game 1 in Denver, Hart made 36 saves and Pavel Dorofeyev scored his league-leading 10th playoff goal in a 4-2 VGK win. Games 2 and 3 followed the same pattern — the Knights scored early, absorbed Colorado's pressure without cracking, and then responded with back-to-back goals in a way that upended the game each time. By the time Game 4 arrived, the Avalanche looked deflated. They gave their best effort — Landeskog's goal tied it briefly — but Stone's return from a lower-body injury that had sidelined him for most of the series gave Vegas the lift it needed.

"Our ultimate goal is to win the Stanley Cup. We know what it takes." — Mark Stone, postgame

Mark Stone's return changed everything

Stone — who missed the first two games of the WCF with a lower-body injury — returned in Game 3 and scored a power-play goal. He scored again in Game 4's opening period, and his presence changed the dynamic of the series entirely. Before he returned, the Avalanche had moments where they controlled large stretches of play. With Stone back, VGK had their best line fully operational and Colorado couldn't find an answer. The captain described the series clinching win simply: "It was probably one of our better games from start to finish."

Avalanche's crushing exit — Makar's honest assessment

For Colorado, the sweep was brutal. Coach Jared Bednar didn't sugarcoat it: "Losing sucks. Losing four straight is worse." Cale Makar, who had returned from injury in time for Game 3, was clearly frustrated: "You work so hard to get to this point, it's obviously frustrating." The Avalanche's record-setting regular season counted for nothing in the end — they were outplayed in every game by a Vegas team that has now made the Stanley Cup Final three times since entering the league in 2017, winning it once (2023).

Who do the Golden Knights play in the Stanley Cup Final?

Vegas will face either the Carolina Hurricanes or the Montreal Canadiens in the Stanley Cup Final. The Hurricanes lead that series 2-1. The Final is expected to begin in the first week of June. Regardless of opponent, the Golden Knights — now the betting favorites to win the Cup — will take the ice with championship experience, a red-hot goaltender, and the best player in the series in Mark Stone at full health.
